What Is an AOG Courier? The Complete Aviation Emergency Delivery Guide

Published: 1 June 2026  |  Author: Same Day Express Editorial Team  |  Category: Aviation & AOG

If you work in aviation procurement, airline operations, or MRO (maintenance, repair, and overhaul), you already know that AOG is the most expensive acronym in the industry. But if you are new to aviation logistics or are a supply chain manager looking for an AOG courier for the first time, this guide explains exactly what AOG means, why it demands a specialist courier, and how the process works in practice.

What Does AOG Mean?

AOG stands for Aircraft on Ground. It is the aviation industry's classification for an aircraft that cannot fly because a critical part has failed or is missing, and the aircraft is effectively grounded until a replacement component arrives. The financial impact is severe and immediate. Industry estimates put the cost of a grounded narrow-body aircraft at between £10,000 and £30,000 per hour in lost revenue, passenger rebooking, crew rescheduling, and cascade disruption across the airline's network. For a wide-body aircraft on a long-haul route, that figure can exceed £100,000 per hour. When an aircraft is AOG, speed of parts delivery is the single factor that determines how quickly it returns to revenue service.

Why Normal Couriers Fail for AOG

Standard parcel networks and next-day courier services are built around consolidation and cost efficiency, not speed and certainty. A typical parcel network collects your item, takes it to a sorting hub, processes it alongside thousands of other items, loads it onto a linehaul vehicle, takes it to a destination hub, sorts it again, and finally dispatches it for delivery. Each hand-off is a potential delay, a potential misroute, and a potential loss. For an AOG situation where every minute costs thousands of pounds, this multi-stage process is unacceptable. Additionally, standard courier drivers do not have airside access experience, do not understand the urgency protocols of aviation operations, and cannot guarantee the level of handling care that high-value aircraft components demand.

What an AOG Specialist Courier Does Differently

An AOG specialist courier operates on a fundamentally different model. When you call with an AOG emergency, a dedicated vehicle is allocated within minutes — not hours, not after the next scheduled collection round, but immediately. That vehicle travels directly from the parts location to the airport with no stops, no sorting, no transfers, and no other cargo on board. Your aircraft component is the only thing in the van. The driver is briefed on the urgency, the airport destination, and any airside delivery requirements. Driver and vehicle details are provided on dispatch, so your operations team knows exactly when the part is en route to the aircraft. A timestamped proof of delivery confirms arrival, giving you the documentation needed for MRO records and return-to-service paperwork.

What AOG Runs Carry

AOG deliveries cover the full spectrum of urgent aircraft components. The most common items include engine components such as turbine blades and auxiliary power units, avionics including navigation systems and communication equipment, landing gear parts like hydraulic actuators and brake assemblies, hydraulic pumps and valves, specialist maintenance tools, ground support equipment, and documentation packs containing airworthiness certificates and maintenance records. Many of these components are high-value, sensitive to shock and vibration, and require careful, secure handling throughout transit.
